# Written Explanation – Robotic Arm Joint Health Monitoring

## 1. Why these safety thresholds
The immediate-stop rules are plain `if` comparisons in `safety.py` (no ML, no imports beyond `math`). Limits are set in `config.py`:
**torque > 130 % of rated** (the usual drive peak rating), **temperature > 95 °C** (above normal grease/housing limits), **vibration > 11 mm/s RMS** (the "damage occurs" zone of ISO 10816). I checked them against my historical data: the worst *failing* joint reached only 100 % torque, 85 °C and 8.8 mm/s, so the limits sit above anything seen before a normal failure. They fire only when something is outside the envelope of even a failing joint, and the ML model is responsible for everything below them. Every single sample is checked (not an average), and a missing or NaN reading is also a stop (fail-safe).

## 2. How the model and the maintenance log combine
The logistic-regression model (torque %, temperature, vibration, hours since service; ~92 % cross-validated accuracy on my 150 synthetic readings) gives a wear probability: LOW < 0.30, MED < 0.60, HIGH ≥ 0.60. The log scanner independently turns recent notes into NONE / MINOR / SEVERE (severe words such as "burning smell" or "grinding" = 3 points, minor words such as "squeak" or "warm" = 1 point; negations like "no grinding noise" and notes older than 14 days are ignored). They combine in a 3×3 table (`decision.py`): both agreeing on something serious (HIGH + SEVERE) gives `EMERGENCY_STOP`; one strong signal alone (HIGH or SEVERE) gives `SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE`; one weak signal (MED or MINOR) gives `MONITOR`, two weak signals give `SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE`; nothing gives `SAFE`. If the model is down, a `SAFE` is downgraded to `MONITOR`, since "safe" needs evidence.

## 3. How the spoofed-sensor defence works
Four checks run on the torque channel: **flatline** (real torque is noisy, a frozen value is not), **replay** (second half of the window copies the first), **implausible step** (a jump no arm could physically make), and **cross-sensor consistency** (a regression learned from history predicts torque from temperature and vibration; if reported torque is more than 3σ ≈ 24 % of rated away, it is flagged; the attacker can't easily fake motor heat). A flagged joint is "degraded": the model is re-run *without* torque. Spoof + any other sign of trouble (model MED/HIGH or any note) → `EMERGENCY_STOP`; spoof alone → `SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE` to inspect the sensor.

**It catches:** frozen values, replayed recordings, sudden drops, and any realistic-looking fake torque that is far from what temperature/vibration imply. **It would not catch:** (a) a *small* lie (e.g. 10 % low) that stays within the 3σ tolerance; (b) a coherent attack that forges torque, temperature *and* vibration together; (c) a slow drift that moves just under the tolerance. Adding a redundant independent sensor (e.g. motor current) would close most of these gaps. Also, a mismatch shows that sensors disagree, not which one lies; I assume torque as the requirement states.
